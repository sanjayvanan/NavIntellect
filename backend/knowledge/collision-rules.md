# Rules of the road (COLREGs, in plain words)

## About the collision regulations
The International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs, 1972) set how ships must behave to avoid collisions. This is a plain-language summary for operators, not the legal text; always check the official rules for anything that matters.

## Lookout and safe speed
Every ship must keep a proper lookout by sight and hearing and by all available means such as radar (Rule 5). Every ship must go at a safe speed for the conditions: visibility, traffic density, how well it can stop and turn, and the sea state (Rule 6). There is no fixed speed limit in the rules themselves; local authorities often set limits in ports, channels and traffic separation schemes.

## Crossing, head-on and overtaking
When two power-driven ships cross with risk of collision, the one that has the other on its own starboard (right) side must keep out of the way (Rule 15). When they meet head-on, both turn to starboard so they pass port side to port side (Rule 14). A ship overtaking another must keep out of its way until well past, whatever the angle (Rule 13). The ship that must keep out of the way is the give-way vessel and acts early and clearly (Rule 16); the other is the stand-on vessel and keeps its course and speed, but must act itself if the give-way vessel does not (Rule 17).

## Who gives way to whom
Rule 18 sets an order of priority. A power-driven ship under way keeps out of the way of ships not under command, ships restricted in their ability to manoeuvre, ships engaged in fishing and sailing ships. A sailing ship keeps out of the way of ships not under command, ships restricted in their ability to manoeuvre and ships engaged in fishing. Ships should avoid impeding a ship constrained by her draught.

## Traffic separation schemes
A traffic separation scheme (TSS) splits a busy strait or approach into one-way lanes (Rule 10). Ships using it must follow their lane in its general direction, join and leave at the ends where possible, and cross only when necessary, at as close to a right angle as possible. Ships should not anchor in a TSS or near its ends, and small boats and fishing boats must not impede ships following a lane. A ship going the wrong way in a lane, drifting or stopping in one is unusual and worth checking.

## Restricted visibility
In fog or heavy rain, ships must go at a safe speed with engines ready to manoeuvre, and a ship that detects another by radar alone must take early action to avoid a close-quarters situation (Rule 19).
